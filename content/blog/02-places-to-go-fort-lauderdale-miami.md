Fort Lauderdale and Miami are only about 30 miles apart, but they feel like two different trips. Fort Lauderdale is laid-back: canals, beach bars and long walks on the sand. Miami is big and busy: art, music, Cuban coffee and neighborhoods that each feel like their own city.

The best part? With a car, you can enjoy both in the same week. Here's where we'd send a friend visiting for the first time.

## Beaches

### Fort Lauderdale and Broward County

- **Fort Lauderdale Beach.** The classic. A wide beach along A1A with the wavy white wall and a promenade that's perfect for a sunset walk. Las Olas Boulevard ends right at the sand.
- **Hugh Taylor Birch State Park.** A quiet, shady park between the Intracoastal and the ocean, just north of the main beach. Walk under the trees, then cross A1A to the beach. There's a small per-vehicle entry fee.
- **Lauderdale-By-The-Sea.** A small beach town with no high-rises on the sand, great snorkeling close to shore, and casual spots to eat on Commercial Boulevard.
- **Hollywood Beach Broadwalk.** About two and a half miles of brick promenade right on the beach, lined with cafés and ice cream shops. Great for bikes, strollers and people-watching.

### Miami and Miami Beach

- **South Beach.** Lummus Park runs along Ocean Drive with the famous colorful lifeguard stands and Art Deco buildings across the street. Go early for a quiet morning, stay for the scene later.
- **Crandon Park, Key Biscayne.** Calm, shallow water and lots of room to spread out. A favorite with families. Parking is right behind the beach.
- **Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park.** At the tip of Key Biscayne, with the Cape Florida Lighthouse. Tours up the lighthouse run several days a week.
- **Haulover Beach Park.** A long, undeveloped stretch of sand between Bal Harbour and Sunny Isles. Locals come here to fly kites.

## Zoos, animals and the Everglades

- **Zoo Miami.** Almost 750 acres and more than 2,000 animals, laid out by region so it feels more like a safari than a zoo. Wear good shoes; there are about four miles of paths. It's in southwest Miami-Dade, so a car makes it easy.
- **Flamingo Gardens, Davie.** A 60-acre botanical garden and wildlife sanctuary west of Fort Lauderdale, home to rescued animals, including, yes, flamingos.
- **Butterfly World, Coconut Creek.** Walk-through aviaries full of butterflies and birds. You can feed the lorikeets (bring a little cash).
- **Everglades airboat ride.** Everglades Holiday Park, west of Fort Lauderdale on Griffin Road, has been running airboat tours for more than 50 years, with alligator shows between rides.
- **Shark Valley, Everglades National Park.** About 25 miles west of Miami on the Tamiami Trail. A two-hour tram tour takes you out to an observation tower over the sawgrass, usually past plenty of alligators. Book ahead in winter.

## Museums and culture

### Miami

- **Pérez Art Museum Miami (PAMM).** Modern and contemporary art in a beautiful building on Biscayne Bay. The shaded porch with hanging gardens is worth the trip on its own.
- **Frost Museum of Science.** Right next to PAMM, with a three-level aquarium and a planetarium. Great for kids and rainy afternoons.
- **Vizcaya Museum & Gardens.** A 1916 Italian-style estate and gardens on the bay in Coconut Grove. It's closed on Tuesdays, and some areas are under preservation work, so check before you go.
- **Wynwood Walls.** Large murals by artists from around the world, surrounded by blocks of galleries, breweries and restaurants.
- **Little Havana.** Walk Calle Ocho (SW 8th Street), watch the domino games at Máximo Gómez Park, and stop for a *cafecito* at a ventanita (walk-up window).

### Fort Lauderdale

- **NSU Art Museum.** On Las Olas Boulevard downtown. Free for anyone 17 and under, and free for everyone on the first Thursday of the month.
- **Bonnet House Museum & Gardens.** An artists' estate hidden between the beach and the Intracoastal, with gardens, mangroves and a view of how Fort Lauderdale looked a century ago. Closed on Mondays.
- **Las Olas Boulevard and the Riverwalk.** Shops, galleries and sidewalk cafés, plus a walk along the New River. The Water Taxi is a fun way to see the canals and the homes along them.

## Local restaurants and hangouts

These are the places locals keep coming back to. Hours change seasonally, so check before you go.

### Miami

- **Versailles, Little Havana.** The best-known Cuban restaurant in Miami, open since 1971. Get a *cafecito* and a *pastelito* at the walk-up window, or sit down for a Cuban sandwich.
- **Ball & Chain, Little Havana.** A Calle Ocho bar with live Latin music and dancing. It first opened in 1935.
- **La Camaronera, Little Havana.** A family seafood spot since 1976, famous for the *pan con minuta*, a fried snapper sandwich.
- **Garcia's, Miami River.** Fresh fish on the riverfront from a family that has been in the seafood business since the 1960s.
- **Joe's Stone Crab, South Beach.** A Miami Beach institution. It opens for stone crab season in the fall and closes for part of the year, so check its site before you go.

### Fort Lauderdale and Hollywood

- **The Mai-Kai, Oakland Park.** A Polynesian restaurant and show that first opened in 1956, back after a major restoration. Order a tiki drink and stay for the show.
- **Rustic Inn Crabhouse.** Garlic crabs, cracked with a wooden mallet at a table covered in newspaper. Messy and worth it.
- **15th Street Fisheries.** Waterfront seafood on the Lauderdale Marina. Feed the tarpon off the dock.
- **LaSpada's Original Hoagies.** A local favorite for big sub sandwiches. Grab one for the beach.
- **Le Tub Saloon, Hollywood.** A quirky, open-air burger spot on the Intracoastal, decorated with old bathtubs.
- **Jaxson's Ice Cream Parlour, Dania Beach.** Open since 1956, with handmade ice cream and sundaes big enough to share.

### Worth the drive south

If you have a free morning, drive to the farms south of Miami. **Robert Is Here** in Homestead is a family-owned fruit stand, open since 1959, known for its fresh fruit milkshakes. **Knaus Berry Farm**, known for its cinnamon rolls, has moved to a new spot in southwest Miami-Dade. It's open in the cooler months, so check the season before you go.

## A few tips for getting around

- **You'll want a car.** The best beaches, the zoo, the Everglades and the farms are spread out, and rideshares add up fast.
- **Parking on Miami Beach is paid and limited.** Use the city garages or the ParkMobile or PayByPhone apps.
- **Tolls are cashless.** Many highways here have no toll booths. With us, tolls are billed at cost at the end of your trip, with no extra toll fees.
- **Go early.** Beaches, the zoo and the Everglades are best in the morning, before the heat and afternoon storms.
